TryTroop Amazon Recovery & Growth Strategy

Prepared by For The Record

Objective

With all three core products back in stock, the remainder of 2026 should focus on rebuilding the customer acquisition and recurring-revenue engines disrupted by the prolonged inventory constraints.

The goal is to progressively return TryTroop toward its historical $40K+ monthly revenue level, while rebuilding search visibility, the subscriber base, and a healthier balance between paid and organic growth.

Current Subscribers (From 700+)

Before going OOS, TryTroop averaged approximately $43K/month, with Lion's Mane generating ~80% of revenue.

The prolonged OOS period significantly reduced search visibility, customer acquisition, and Subscribe & Save, with the subscriber base declining from 700+ to ~55.

All three core products are now back in stock, but the brand needs to rebuild the visibility, customer base, and recurring revenue that previously supported its historical sales level.

Key Issues & Blockers
  • Search exposure remains significantly below the pre-OOS baseline, particularly for Lion's Mane.
  • Lion's Mane has lost Subscribe & Save eligibility, limiting subscriber acquisition on the Hero ASIN.
  • The subscriber base has declined by more than 90%.
  • Historical growth was highly PPC-dependent and should not simply be recreated at the same economics.
  • Inventory stability remains critical to protecting the recovery.
Growth Area 1: Rebuild the Foundation

The first priority is to protect inventory availability and restore Subscribe & Save eligibility for the Hero ASIN, Lion's Mane Gummies, to support sustainable growth.

Protect Inventory Availability

Inventory instability was the root cause of the decline and contributed to lost search visibility, sales velocity, subscribers, and S&S eligibility.

Key Actions
  • Maintain sufficient inventory coverage across all three core ASINs to support the year-end recovery.
  • Align advertising, promotional activity, and subscriber acquisition with inventory availability.
  • Establish stronger inventory safeguards to prevent another OOS period from interrupting the recovery.
Restore Lion's Mane Subscribe & Save Eligibility

Lion's Mane is currently unable to acquire new subscribers through Sub&Save, creating a significant blocker for the Hero ASIN.

Key Actions
  • Work with Amazon to restore Lion's Mane Sub&Save eligibility now that inventory has stabilized.
  • Once reinstated, activate the planned 15% Sub&Save incentive to accelerate subscriber acquisition.
  • Protect Sub&Save eligibility through consistent inventory availability going forward.

Growth Area 3: Rebuild Recurring Revenue

TryTroop historically reached 700+ active subscriptions, compared with approximately 55 today.

Subscribers also generated significantly greater customer value than one-time purchasers, making recurring revenue a major component of the historical business model.

Rebuild Subscribe & Save

Once Sub&Save is restored across the core catalog:

  • Use strong Sub&Save incentives to accelerate subscriber acquisition.
  • Prioritize Lion's Mane due to its historical scale and role as the primary acquisition product.
  • Track subscriber growth and retention alongside revenue recovery.
  • Maintain sufficient inventory to protect recurring deliveries and prevent further subscriber loss.

The historical 700+ subscriber base should serve as a long-term recovery benchmark, rather than an immediate year-end target.

Protect Subscriber Retention

Historical Sub&Save performance demonstrates that customers who remain subscribed become increasingly valuable over time.

The objective should therefore extend beyond acquiring subscriptions to rebuilding a long-term recurring customer base.

Prioritize:

  • New subscriber growth
  • 60/90-day retention
  • Growth in longer-term subscribers
  • Subscriber revenue
  • Consistent Sub&Save fulfillment

PPC Approach

Advertising has been deliberately restrained through most of 2026. Budgets were capped during the OOS period to protect remaining inventory, campaigns restarted at a slow pace as stock arrived, and Lion's Mane advertising only resumed meaningfully once its inventory landed in August. Recent performance therefore reflects a warm-up period rather than a true baseline.

The current picture is consistent with a brand that has lost its search position. Branded searches still convert strongly and remain efficient. Non branded searches, which represent the majority of spend, are converting well below the level required to support them. Customers who already know TryTroop continue to buy. The account is no longer winning cold traffic efficiently.

For the record:

Rebuilding non branded acquisition is the objective. It cannot simply be scaled at today's economics.

PPC Objective

Advertising's role through year end is to rebuild non branded visibility, sales velocity and organic ranking on the priority Lion's Mane search terms, rather than to hit a short-term efficiency target.

Two guardrails apply:

  • Ranking focused activity may deliberately run above break even on defined keywords for a defined period. All other activity should be working toward profitability.
  • TACOS is the primary measure of progress, not ACOS. Lion's Mane historically ran at approximately 10% TACOS. A progressive return toward that level, with revenue growing at the same time, is the correct definition of success.
Phase 1: Restore Efficiency and Structure

Advertising spend is currently spread across a very large number of campaigns and keywords, the majority of which generate little or no activity. Spend is not constrained by budget. It is constrained by structure, relevance and bidding. Before investment is increased, the account needs to be made optimisable.

A meaningful share of spend is also reaching traffic unrelated to the products, particularly cannabis and confectionery search terms. These attract clicks at well above average rates and produce almost no sales, making them the fastest available efficiency gain.

Key Actions
  • Apply a structural negative keyword layer to eliminate irrelevant traffic across the account.
  • Consolidate the campaign structure, concentrating spend into fewer campaigns that accumulate enough data to be optimised and measured.
  • Review the existing negative keyword layer to confirm no priority or branded search terms are being unintentionally blocked.
  • Bring non-branded bids back in line with current conversion performance.
  • Fully fund branded and brand-defence coverage, which remains efficient and is under-invested during a period when the brand is rebuilding recognition.
  • Rebuild the priority Lion's Mane campaigns around the defined Search Term List, structured so ranking progress on each term can be measured individually.
Phase 2: Rebuild Non-Branded Visibility

Once structure and efficiency are corrected, investment can scale behind the terms demonstrating recovery.

Key Actions
  • Scale investment behind high-intent Lion's Mane product searches where visibility is available but position remains weak.
  • Expand into benefit-based acquisition terms only where high-intent performance supports it.
  • Grow competitor and category targeting, currently the strongest non-branded activity in the account and a faster route back to visibility while organic ranking recovers.
  • Reactivate Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display selectively for top-of-search coverage, brand defence and retargeting. Both are effectively dormant today.
  • Increase Reishi investment selectively as visibility recovers, maintaining Super 6 at controlled levels.
  • Align paid acquisition with the Sub&Save incentive once Lion's Mane eligibility is restored, so advertising builds recurring revenue rather than one-time purchases.
Phase 3: Scale and Improve Efficiency
Key Actions
  • Scale proven non-branded acquisition where organic ranking is demonstrably improving.
  • Progressively reduce paid support on terms where organic position has been restored, increasing organic contribution.
  • Improve TACoS through revenue growth and organic recovery rather than through spend reduction.
  • Maintain ranking pressure on core Lion's Mane terms through the year-end period.
  • Keep advertising aligned to inventory coverage at all times, reducing spend ahead of any stock risk rather than after it.
Dependencies
  • Advertising cannot deliver the recovery alone. Three factors outside PPC determine what it can achieve.
  • Listing conversion is the largest single constraint. Lion's Mane is winning search impressions on its priority terms but converting them into clicks and sales at well below the level required. Strengthening search-result recognition is a prerequisite for scaling non-branded investment, not a parallel workstream.
  • Inventory stability determines whether investment compounds. Every previous ramp in this account was interrupted by stock.
  • Sub&Save eligibility determines the value of each acquired customer. Until Lion's Mane can acquire subscribers, advertising is buying one-time purchasers at a cost structure that only makes sense against recurring revenue.
Measurement

PPC will be reported monthly against the recovery, prioritising:

  • TACOS trend as the primary efficiency measure.
  • Organic ranking and visibility on the priority Search Term List.
  • Non-branded ad sales and orders as the customer acquisition measure.
  • Conversion performance on priority search terms as the listing health signal.
  • Subscriber acquisition attributable to paid traffic.
  • Weekly performance currently moves on very small order volumes, so short-term swings should be treated as noise. Four week trends are the meaningful read.
